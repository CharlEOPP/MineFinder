# 0002: Use EPSG:26913 everywhere and reject anything else at the boundary

**Decision:** Every geometry and raster in the project uses **EPSG:26913 (NAD83 / UTM zone 13N)**, in metres. Data gets reprojected once, when it first enters (`normalize`). After that, code *refuses* any other CRS instead of quietly converting.

## In plain words

A coordinate like `(456564, 4435895)` is meaningless until you know which "map grid" it's on. That grid is the **CRS** (coordinate reference system). The same physical spot near Ward is:

- `(-105.5094, 40.0722)` in EPSG:4326, which is degrees of longitude and latitude
- about `(456564, 4435895)` in EPSG:26913, which is metres east and north of a reference point

We want metres for two reasons:
1. **Distances are simple.** "Are these two mine records within 50 m?" is just the Pythagorean theorem in UTM. In degrees, one degree of longitude is about 85 km here but about 111 km at the equator, so "50 m" isn't a fixed number.
2. **USGS 3DEP LiDAR for Colorado is delivered in UTM 13N on NAD83.** Matching the source data means the 1 m DEM never gets resampled, and every resample blurs exactly the small pits and dumps we're trying to detect.

### The sneaky one: EPSG:32613

EPSG:32613 is *also* "UTM zone 13N", but on the **WGS84** datum instead of NAD83. A datum is the model of Earth's shape the grid is pinned to. The two datums differ by about 1–2 m in Colorado today. Coordinates in either one look identical at a glance, so mixing them doesn't crash anything. It just shifts every label a metre or two off the feature it labels. On a 1 m DEM, that puts the label pixel next to the shaft instead of on it. That's why the test rejects `EPSG:32613` explicitly.

## Formally

- **Projected CRS** (planar x/y in linear units) vs **geographic CRS** (angles on an ellipsoid). Euclidean distance and area are only meaningful in a projected CRS, and in UTM scale distortion is under 0.1% within a zone.
- **Datum**: the reference frame (ellipsoid plus anchoring). NAD83 is fixed to the North American plate, while WGS84 tracks the global frame, so they drift apart about 1–2 cm per year.
- **Validate at the boundary, trust inside.** Reproject once at ingest (`normalize`), and assert the CRS everywhere else (`load_aoi`, `merge_labels`). An assert that fails loudly is better than an implicit `to_crs()` deep in the pipeline that hides a bug.
- We compare `pyproj.CRS` objects (`crs == PROJECT_CRS`), not strings, so `"EPSG:26913"`, `"epsg:26913"` and an equivalent WKT all match, and anything else doesn't.

## Why this and not the alternatives

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| EPSG:4326 everywhere | Distances are in degrees, so every buffer and dedupe radius would be wrong |
| EPSG:32613 (WGS84 UTM) | Off by about 1–2 m from the LiDAR's native datum, so we'd be resampling for no gain |
| Colorado State Plane | Feet, three zones across the state, and a mismatch with 3DEP delivery |
| Auto-reproject anywhere | Hides mistakes, so a wrong CRS becomes silently wrong results |

## Where it lives

- `src/minefinder/aoi.py` → `PROJECT_CRS`, and the check in `load_aoi`
- `src/minefinder/acquire/labels.py` → `normalize` reprojects, `merge_labels` refuses mismatches
- Tests: `test_rejects_any_crs_other_than_26913`, `test_normalize_reprojects_from_geographic`, `test_normalize_refuses_missing_crs`, `test_merge_refuses_mixed_crs`

## Revisit when

The statewide scale-up reaches western Colorado, which is in UTM **zone 12**. Then we either tile per zone or switch to an equal-area CRS like EPSG:5070 for the statewide index. That gets its own decision record.
